# Running a night with a watch

For a team going out on patrol with one person holding the watch for them: what each person sets
up once, what they do each night, and what the app does not do. It covers the app only. What to do
for somebody on the street is the team's own knowledge, and NavCom does not write it.

## Once, before the first night

**Everyone**

1. Open `navcom.app/terminal/` and press **Create your profile**. Any name you like.
2. Make a backup on **Backup**, and keep it somewhere that is not this phone.
3. On **Setup**, under **Someone to call**, add the person holding the watch, with their phone number. A
   Distress then opens a text to them, ready to send, which goes over the phone network even when
   the app cannot reach anything.
4. Open your city's area under **Cached directory** once, so its places stay on the phone with no signal.
5. For a night in New York, open **Map** on Status and press **Save … for tonight** on wifi: the borough
   you are in, or the New Jersey side (13 to 43 MB). Its streets then draw with no signal. Patrolling two,
   save the other under **Other areas**.

**The person holding the watch**

1. Use a laptop or a tablet that stays on power.
2. **Watch → Start a watch on this phone.**
3. If a second person will relieve you, do this now, before anyone scans the code:
   - press **Show the watch key** and let them scan it under **Or join one**
   - open **This phone's holder key** on their screen, and scan it under **Other holders** on yours
   - give them a `can take watch` vouch, which they claim on **Standing**

   Not in the same room: press the Copy button beside each instead, send it over a channel you both trust,
   such as Signal, and paste it in the same place on the other screen. Whoever has the watch key can
   answer as the watch, so send it to nobody else.
4. Another watch can hold other teams on the same night: they start their own watch here, and their
   teams scan their code instead. The two boards are separate.
5. With more than one team out, type them under **Teams tonight**, so sign-on offers them.
6. Hold up **Code for operators**, or press **Copy the code** and send it to operators who are elsewhere.

**Everyone else, after that**

On **Setup**, under **Watch — if somebody gave you one**, press **Scan a watch code** and scan it. If a holder is
added later, everyone scans the new code again: until they do, the new holder cannot read anything
they send.

**The day before**, everyone reloads NavCom once with signal. On the night, **Older build** under
Who is out names anybody whose phone has not, and they should reload before going out.

**A dry run.** Before the real night, with both holders and at least two phones on two different
networks (one on wifi, one on cell data), about forty-five minutes. Each line says what should
happen. If it does not, stop and tell whoever looks after NavCom.

1. Both holders hold the watch. Each phone signs on with its team. Both holders see both phones.
2. A phone checks in, then asks a question. Both holders answer it at once: the phone shows an answer.
3. A phone sends an Assist marked *now*, and a holder passes it to the nearest 3. A phone it reached
   presses **I can go**.
4. Put one phone in airplane mode, then tell everyone out something. When the phone comes back, it
   has it too.
5. A phone raises one Distress that says it is a test. While it sounds, reload one holder's laptop.
   The other holder answers it, and the phone names them.
6. The holder whose laptop reloaded presses **Stand down**, then holds the watch again. The phones
   read *On station* the whole time.
7. On a phone, save tonight's borough, then airplane mode, then **Map**: the streets still draw.
8. Lock a signed-on iPhone for five minutes. Nothing alarms, and the watch says when it last heard it.
9. One holder stands down: the phones still read *On station*. Then the other: the phones read
   *Dark*, and Distress says there is no watch to send it to.

## Each night, going out

- **Sign on.** An area (a neighbourhood, not an address), your team if several are out together,
  how long, how often you will check in, and whether to share where you are. *Roughly* is to about
  500 m. The team you gave is offered again next time.
- **Check in** at the interval you gave. Past it, the watch may ask you to; nothing else happens.
- **Query** asks the watch. A person answers, so it can take a minute. *Waiting for the watch* shows
  on Query and Status, and you can use other screens meanwhile.
- **Assist** asks for another person: *soon* or *now*.
- **Distress** keeps sending until a person answers. Use it for an emergency.
- **Map**, on Status, shows you, the peers you paired with who share where they are, and where you are
  going. **Go there**, beside an answer from a directory place, opens it; **Directions** hands it to your
  phone's map app.
- **Watch says**, on Status and along the foot of every other screen, is what the watch told
  everybody out. Only the newest is kept, and nothing needs an answer.
- **Your peers**, on Status, are the people you paired with: who is out, and a map link for any of
  them sharing where they are.
- **Passed on**, on Status, is somebody else's Assist that the watch passed to you. It is an offer:
  **I can go** tells the watch in one tap.
- **Playbook**, on Status, says what to press and who the city says to call for the nights a patrol
  meets most: somebody needing somewhere warm, no signal, somebody saying you must register with them,
  a rumour about another operator. It works with no signal. It never says how to treat somebody: that
  is the team's own training.
- **Stand down** when you are home.

## Each night, holding the watch

- **Power** on the Watch screen says whether this laptop is charging, and how much battery is left.
- **Map**, beside Who is out, shows everybody on the board who shares where they are, on the streets of
  all five boroughs and the New Jersey side, or a radar. **Follow** keeps one in the middle. On the
  laptop, save every area under **Other areas** (139 MB together).
- Open **Watch** and keep it open. While you hold the watch it keeps the screen on and sounds a
  Distress until you answer it or press Silence. Touch the screen once so the browser lets it play.
- **Hold to take the watch.** *Published: Yes* means operators can see you.
- **Who is out** shows Distress first, then anybody past their time, then whoever is due next.
  Each row says their team, when they are due and when you last heard from them, and has a map
  link if they share where they are. Two people with the same name each show the start of their
  key. With more than one team out, the team buttons narrow the list, and what is waiting on you,
  to the teams you pick; a Distress and anybody past their time stay on it, and what waits on the
  other teams is named under **Also waiting**.
- **Two holding together:** keep both Watch screens open and split the teams between you out loud,
  each picking their own. An answer from either of you clears it on both screens.
- **Answering:** **Add a place** opens the area nearest the person asking. The place you pick goes
  into the answer, and the answer shows where it came from and when it was last checked.
- **Past their time:** **Ask … to check in**. They see it silently, and nobody else does.
- **An Assist:** **Pass to the others out** sends it to everyone else on the board, or to the teams
  you picked, in your words. **Pass to the nearest 3** sends it to the three closest of those who
  share where they are, when the person asking shares too. Nobody in Distress is sent one.
- **Telling everyone:** **Tell everyone out**, under Who is out, sends what is happening to everyone
  on the board, or to the teams you picked. Places and conditions, not orders and not people. It
  stands until you clear it or say something new: anybody who signs on later, or was out of signal,
  is told it the next time their phone is heard.
- **At the end:** stand down. **Tonight** lists what the watch did; copy it out for whoever reviews
  the night. A panic wipe deletes it.

## What it does not do

- **Nothing sounds once the Watch screen is closed or the device sleeps.** No one else is paged.
  If you step away, hand over first.
- **A locked iPhone sends nothing.** The app has to be open to send a Distress. The text to the
  person you call is the fallback.
- **An answer can be lost in a tunnel.** Answers are not kept, so one sent while somebody had no
  signal never arrives. Their screen says *No answer heard* when the wait ends; ask again.
- **The directory has not been checked by a person.** Most places come from public listings.
  Call before you send anybody, and correct what you know from the area screen.
- **The board is only what this device has heard.** Reload it, or take over from someone else, and
  it starts empty until each operator's phone tells it again.
